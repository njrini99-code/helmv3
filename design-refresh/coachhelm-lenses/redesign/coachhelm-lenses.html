CoachHelm · coach view and player view

One file per player, seen through two lenses

Today a coach taps a name chip to see one read, then opens a second page for the rest and a third for Standing. The player gets those same pages with “you” swapped in. This gives every player one file that holds everything CoachHelm knows about them, and lets the coach flip it to see exactly what the player sees.

  1. One push to everything about a player. Every read, the plan, the strokes chart, the last round against the field and the coach's own notes on one screen. No chip strip, no second page.
  2. Assign as focus shows the handshake. Suggested, Shown and Accepted, read from rows the engine already writes: the focus area and the insight exposure ledger (Shown is the first time the read was drawn on the player's own CoachHelm, not a tap).
  3. “As Eli sees it.” A lens switch on the coach's file shows the player's own screen, with the coach-only cards folded away. The coach never has to guess what reached the player.
  4. The player's Board becomes “Your week”. One focus with its drill, what is working, the last round against the field, and progress on the goal. Words first, the number one tap away.
  5. Four things the engine already computes finally reach the screen: the same-day field adjustment, the coach's plan for the player, the track record with this player, and goal check-ins.

Names and figures in the prototype are example data from Demo University Golf, drawn in the real shapes from data/coachhelm-shape.ts, coachhelm-dive-shape.ts and coachhelm-standing-shape.ts. Use the bar above to switch coach or player, phone or desktop, theme, text size, Reduce Motion and the state. Version 6, 3 Nov: the More menu is drawn; every motion now runs on the app’s own primitives (pushed screen, sheet, menu, press, Swap, settle spring), cards and rows take a tint instead of a scale, and chart bars are drawn at rest. Version 5 settled Nick's calls: players compare against the PGA Tour and team averages only. Version 6 follows the shared acceptance rules: Reduce Motion is the app's 180ms fade, the feature card is flat green, and the More menu, skeletons and corrections match today's app.

Where it lives

From three pages to one push

What Nick called confusing is the path, not the cards. On phone the coach scrolls a chip strip to find a player, reads one top insight, then follows “All of Eli's reads” to the Deep dive and switches again to Standing. Everything below the first read is two hops away, and the coach's own context for that player lives on Roster.

The evidence rule holds everywhere: no figure without its sample, window and read word beside it, and every read opens a sheet with the comparison, the rounds behind it, the holes where the counted path happened, and why CoachHelm believes it. The lens

“As Eli sees it”

The segmented control under the bar flips the file into the player's own screen. Shared cards stay where they are and change voice (“Eli is averaging” becomes “You're averaging”). Coach-only cards fold away. Firmness drops from the claim ladder word to the read word the player feed already uses. Named teammates become the team average.

This is the answer to “a lot of the CoachHelm data isn't making it in there” from the other side: the coach can check in one tap what the player actually got, instead of trusting that a share went through.

Motion
The chip slides on the app’s settle spring (417ms). Cards this lens hides fold away on the 462ms smooth spring and fade over 180ms; cards it shows fold in and fade over 260ms. Shared cards change their words in place with today’s Swap: the old copy fades over 180ms while the new one fades in over 260ms and rises 6px. The preview pill folds in the same way. Reduce Motion: the lens and the pill take the app’s 180ms opacity fade (CH_FADE_MS) and nothing moves; shared cards change at once, as today’s Swap does.
Data
No new read. The lens draws the player components with PLAYER_VOICE from the data the coach's page already loaded, minus the coach-only fields.

The player's CoachHelm

Your week: one thing, why, and how it's going

Switch the bar to Player. The player's Board keeps its place (green bar, “CoachHelm ⌄”, Board · Standing · Deep dive) but leads with a plain-language week, the way TrainingPeaks turns numbers into state words and Arccos leads with strengths. The number is always one tap away, never hidden.

Research

What we borrowed, and from whom

  • TrainingPeaksThe coach sees numbers with a compliance colour; the athlete sees a plain state word (“Fresh”, “Overloading”) with a published range and a tap for the number. Coach notes carry an eye toggle for shown or hidden. Taken: words first for the player, numbers first for the coach.
    help.trainingpeaks.com, Athlete Home
  • Clippd“What To Work On” ranks areas by importance, opportunity and trend, and the player sees the same list as their roadmap. The coach is always included in anything a player shares. Taken: one list, two framings.
    clippd.com, What To Work On
  • ArccosThree strengths beside three weaknesses; a focus area the player can lock and track over time. Taken: What's working on the player's week, and the goal line.
    golf.com, Arccos 2026 update
  • HudlEvery stat in a report opens its clip. Taken: every number opens its rounds and holes.
    hudl.com, Assist
  • SkillestFeedback lives on the lesson with one follow-up assignment. Taken: the focus carries its drill and the coach's name.
    skillest.com, online lessons
  • AvoidedA colour with no way into its evidence (TrainingPeaks' own help warns about it), raw strokes gained dumped on players (Golf Monthly on Clippd), and a coach view that only works one player at a time (Arccos' coach dashboard).

Decided

Nick's four calls, settled 31 Oct

Nick: “All four on Lenses.” These are now part of the design, and the build package records them.

  • 1. One file per player.

    It replaces the board's single selected card and the coach's separate Deep dive and Standing pages. Old addresses land on the matching card. The team-view lane still owns what the board itself looks like; this only changes where a player row goes.

    Yes, Oct 31
  • 2. The “As Eli sees it” lens on the coach's file.

    A preview of the player's screen, drawn from data already loaded. No new read, and the player never sees a coach-only field.

    Yes, Oct 31
  • 3. The coach's plan for the player, coach only, in its own words.

    Aim (Breakout, Develop, Maintain, Bubble, Rehab), alert level, what to watch and the note. Today only Roster reads it; RLS keeps it coach-only.

    Yes, Oct 31
  • 4. Players see the team average, never teammates by name.

    Coaches keep named comparisons. Every new card follows this, the same-day field included: the player sees the field's average only. Teammates' dots are coach only, because in a field of 3 to 5 a player could tell whose dot is whose.

    Yes, Oct 31

Build order

PR-sized, each behind its own flag, nothing deployed

The build package is in the project's files under clubhouse-handoff/design-refresh/coachhelm-lenses/: the fidelity spec, the file-by-file plan, the data checks and the capture script.

  1. Player file shell and route. ?view=player&player=, redirects from the coach's deep-dive and standing addresses, phone push and desktop rail. Existing cards moved in.
  2. Plan card with the handshake. Focus areas plus the exposure ledger for Shown; goal snapshots for the line.
  3. Last round in context. A server read over the field-day loader for the newest round; both lenses.
  4. Coach-only cards. Intent (the Roster read, reused) and the per-player track record.
  5. The lens. Player components in PLAYER_VOICE over the coach's loaded data.
  6. Your week for the player. PlayerBoard.tsx rebuilt to the same cards.

Prep only: no deploys, migrations or flag flips. Nothing here changes what shot tracking collects. No accent left-border cards anywhere. Text contrast follows DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md tokens (4.9:1 or better in light and dark).
