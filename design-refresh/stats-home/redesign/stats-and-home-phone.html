Stats, rebuilt to read like iPhone

My stats, team Stats and the bottom of player Home, built to the shared Clubhouse design language. Same stats the app records today, same rule for when a change is coloured. Tap the phone: switch the window, light and dark, Reduce Motion and Text Size, tap any card to open its detail page, and drag across the scoring chart.

  • Summary first, detail on tapEach tab opens on calm cards, one figure and one visual each. Tap a card and its full page slides in with the rest of the visuals, the way production's stats tiles open into their drills, now built for the phone. Every stat is still there.
  • Today's top bar, keptThe real green bar: the section switcher with its caret and dot on the left, the bell on the right, and the ivory sheet tucked under it. A detail page keeps the same bar with a back button.
  • One flat page#F2F0E9 in light, #0E120F in dark. No wash and no gradient behind text. Section names sit on the page in bold; everything else lives on a card.
  • Charts you can read, in amberBars on visible tracks, a 3:1 zero rule and Tour tick, steady lines at 5.6:1 instead of 2.9:1, and amber in place of brown for every loss.

Where each piece lives

Tap a note to jump the phone to that piece. Each one names today's file and what changes in it.

    What's on the phone today

    Your four screenshots, measured. Most body text actually passes 4.5:1. The problem is structure: titles and captions float on a tinted, shifting page between cards that barely separate from it, and the chart marks around them fall under 3:1.

    Contrast, checked

    Worked out live from the phone's own colour values, in both themes, with the WCAG 2 formula, against the surface each piece actually sits on. Text needs 4.5:1. Bars, lines, dots, ticks and rules need 3:1. Feature card pairs are also checked at the card's lightest point, under its soft light.

    Motion

    From the design language's one motion set. Data never overshoots, nothing counts up, nothing staggers in on load, and only transform and opacity animate. With Reduce Motion on, nothing travels: changes cross-fade in 120 ms.

    Beyond the design language

    Everything else on the phone uses the language's tokens and rules as written. These are the pieces I added; each needs a nod before the build brief takes it.

    AdditionValueWhy
    --ch-chart-gain-fill, --ch-chart-loss-fill#1F6B45 / #4FA877 · #B86F0C / #E09A35Bars need a brighter amber than text amber to read as amber, not brown, at 3:1 or better. Text keeps --ch-gain and --ch-loss.
    --ch-chart-flat, --ch-chart-track#6B675F / #A39D90 · #EBE5D8 / #2C322DA neutral line that clears 3:1 when nothing changed, and a track you can see on a card.
    Feature card chart tonesIvory text, mint #8FD3AA, amber #EDB867, coral #F49A8DThe green feature card holds the scoring chart, so its marks need light tones that pass on the green, checked at its lightest point.
    Chip tints as fixed colours10% light, 16% darkDark mode needs a stronger tint to read as a chip; fixed values keep the ratios checkable.
    --ch-type-figure-tile28/1, 600, width 92Tiles and Home legs carry one number each, smaller than the 40 pt hero.
    Pushed detail pagesOne per cardThe language has no drill-down on phone. Production's stats board already opens each tile into a drill; this is that pattern as an iPhone push, so the summary can stay light without dropping anything.
    Section names on the page22/28, 700Overview, Your game, Rounds and Development group the cards. The language puts titles inside cards; these are the only words left on the page, in full ink.
    Small visualsTrend ribbon, round ticker, mini rails, goal ringsEach summary card needs one picture that reads at a glance; these reuse the chart tones above and the same Tour tick.
    Scoring line morph360 ms, --ch-easeThe language says chart marks never animate on re-render. This is the one exception: when you change the window, the line glides to its new shape so you can see what moved. Everything else cross-fades.
