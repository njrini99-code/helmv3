GolfHelm · Clubhouse · Messages

Messages with depth: lifted bubbles, glass controls, and motion you can feel

Today the header, thread and composer are three flat slabs, and ivory bubbles melt into an ivory page. This keeps Clubhouse’s greens, ivory, champagne and Instrument Sans, and builds the three layers iMessage, WhatsApp and Claude use: the conversation, bubbles lifted off it, and glass controls floating above.

  1. Today’s top bar stays, with a floating glass composer below. The green bar, the Messages switcher and the bell are exactly as they are in the app now. The thread slides under the bar’s rounded lip and blurs under the composer.
  2. Bubbles that sit above one flat page. Received bubbles are paper with a hairline and a soft shadow; yours are Clubhouse green with a lit top edge. Tails only on the last bubble of a run, 2 px between bubbles in a run, 10 px between runs.
  3. Sending is physical. Your message rises out of the composer on a spring, the thread glides up to make room, and a reply opens out of the typing bubble.
  4. Hold any message. It lifts over a blurred thread with a glass reaction bar and menu beside it. Seen by lives there for your own messages in a group.
  5. Less chrome in the thread. Times hide until you drag left, a date pill floats while you scroll, reactions clip to the bubble’s corner, and a round jump button carries the unread count.
  6. Polls show people. Voter avatars on each choice, bars that glide to new shares, and See votes grouped by choice with who hasn’t voted.

Top bar: unchanged. On the phone, every Messages screen keeps today’s green bar: the section switcher on the left, the bell on the right, the parchment sheet’s rounded lip below. Desktop keeps today’s sidebar and glass top bar. The device on the left is live in light and dark, at three text sizes and with Reduce Motion. Each section below has Play buttons and the exact spring values for code.

Motion system

Six springs, three of them already in Clubhouse

Clubhouse already has smooth, settle and reveal in lib/motion.ts. Pushes and sheets use the shared smooth spring at 462 ms, as the Clubhouse design language sets. Messages adds three for moments only chat has: send, pop and lift. Values are framer’s visualDuration and bounce. Stiffness and damping use chSpring’s own conversion, so code can pass them straight in. The SwiftUI duration is 1.2 × visualDuration under that conversion.

Two house rules stay. No count-ups: a vote count or reaction count changes at once while its bar or badge moves. No staggers: rows that arrive together move together. The typing dots’ wave is one looping element, not a list.

Conversation list

Pinned faces up top, quiet rows below

The team channel is always pinned first, and you can pin up to two more people; each pinned face shows its latest line above it. Rows lose the boxed sections: an unread dot, name, time and two lines of preview. Typing dots show inside an open conversation, where Clubhouse already hears them.

Typing and sending

The message leaves from where you typed it

One glass pill holds +, the field and the key. Nothing sits on the right while the field is empty; the green send key springs in the moment you type. The field grows a line at a time. On send, the bubble rises from the field to its place, and the thread above glides up on the same spring instead of jumping, the way Telegram moves the bubble on its own layer above the list.

Holding a message

Lift, blur, and the actions where your thumb is

Hold a bubble: it sinks slightly, then lifts at 450 ms with a haptic while the thread blurs behind it. The reaction bar sits above the bubble and the menu below, both glass, both growing out of the bubble’s edge. Same actions as today: Reply, Copy, Edit, Delete, and on your own message in a group, Seen by with faces. On desktop, hovering a message shows a small bar with three reactions, Reply and More.

Reactions stop taking their own row. They sit on the bubble’s top corner, white with green glyphs, and turn solid green when one is yours. Double-tap a message to Like it.

Jump to latest

A round key that knows what you missed

Bubbles and avatars

The numbers behind the look

Contrast

Every text pair, light and dark

Checked against WCAG 2.2: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for marks like the unread dot. Glass is measured as the color it settles on over the page.

Design language

Built to the Clubhouse design language

Messages follows the shared refresh: one flat page, text on surfaces, one card style with depth, the checked text colors, 16 px edges, sentence case, and the shared springs with Reduce Motion. Chat needs a few things the language doesn’t cover yet. These are added in its spirit, for the design lead to fold in.

Where it lands

Each piece, mapped to today’s code

Design only. No app code, PR or deploy came from this page. Rules kept: no accent left-border cards anywhere; text follows iPhone Text Size; page edge 16 px (20 on large phones); nothing changes what shot tracking collects.

What we borrowed

From iMessage, Telegram, WhatsApp, Slack and Claude
