CoachHelm builds the practice

A coach types "build Thursday's practice around putting" and CoachHelm reads the calendar, the team's rounds, putting by distance, class times and the drill bank, then builds the plan block by block. Every block says why, from real data. The coach edits, saves a draft, and publishes with one confirm that names who is told. The same helper sits inside the practice plan builder.

Scene 1 · CoachHelm › Ask · iPhone

It asks first, then builds the plan in front of you

Press Play. CoachHelm checks the calendar, then asks a few quick questions with its best guesses already picked. Once the coach taps Build plan, each read shows a small picture of what it found, and the plan writes itself block by block. Tap any block to edit it, tap a source chip to see the numbers, then try Save and Publish.

1. It asks before it builds

After one quick read of the calendar, CoachHelm asks "What should Thursday cover?" in one card with three groups: how long, what to work on (pick any) and who. It never asks what the request already said (Thursday), and its best guesses come ticked with where they came from ("Your stats point at the first two"), so most coaches just tap Build the plan. The card then folds into the coach's own message ("3 h · short putts, lag, up and down, holes 1 to 6 · everyone") and the work carries on. The plan follows the answers: picking "On course, holes 1 to 6" is why holes 1 to 6 are in it. Same card as the top-bar chat and the builder's New plan sheet.

2. Every read shows what it found

The steps follow the shared CoachHelm kit used by Ask and the top-bar chat: a breathing dot while a read runs, a check that draws in, and one line of what it found. Here each read also draws a small picture once it lands: Thursday lit on the week with Hargrove on Monday, strokes gained by area with putting in amber, make rate by distance against the Tour tick, the class clash on a timeline, and the drills it picked fanning out of the bank. When the plan is done the steps fold to "6 steps · 9s".

3. The plan writes itself, block by block

A card in the thread with the plan's title and the day strip (block width is minutes, like TrainingPeaks). It opens with a quiet outline of all six blocks; a soft green ring moves down them as each one is written: time first, the title typing in, then place, drills and the Why. The strip grows a segment as each block lands, and the card settles when it is done. Each block shows time, length, place and drill, then a Why panel with its Aim and Because and the source chips. Routine blocks (warm-up, a move, the wrap) say "Routine" and claim nothing.

4. Change it by asking or by touching it

  • Chips under the plan: "Make it 2 hours", "More pressure", "Lighter before Monday". A change marks the blocks it touched with "Changed" and keeps the old version one tap back.
  • Tap a block: a sheet with its length stepper, place, coach, drill with Swap, its Why, and "Ask about this block".
  • Swap a drill: three drills from the bank that fit the same gap, each with its own because.

5. Save to any folder. Publish asks once.

The plan card has a "Save to Practice plans ⌄" row. Tapping it opens the folders right inside the card, with New folder at the bottom, which turns into a name field with Create; the new folder lands at the top, already chosen. Save draft turns into a Saving capsule, the plan drops into that folder (it squashes and its count ticks up), the card edge flashes green once, and the receipt says "Plan saved · Documents · Hargrove week" with Open and an 8 second Undo. Publish shows who is told with faces, the exact notification, the team chat switch and the folder. "On confirm" says the 11 people hear only when Undo closes. Then the same finishing beats: capsule, the plan files into its folder, edge flash, the check draws itself, 8 second Undo, then everyone lights up as they are told.

Scene 2 · CoachHelm › Ask · Mac

The plan opens beside the chat, as the page you print

On desktop the thread keeps a compact plan card and the plan builds on the right as the printed page itself, the way Claude opens a PDF. It reads like a real practice sheet: date block, focus, time, place and coaches, the three groups up top, then a schedule where every block says who is where. The putting rotation is a small grid of stations by turn with each group's letter, the course block shows which hole each group starts on, and everything else says Everyone. The coach copy adds one short Why line per block; players get the same sheet without them. Bring is a checklist and Notes has lines to write on. The page stays light in dark mode, because that is what prints.

Scene 3 · Practice › Plan format · iPhone

Upload a plan you like, and every plan comes out that way

Coaches already have a sheet they hand out. They upload one, CoachHelm reads its layout once, and from then on every plan from Ask and the builder comes out in that layout. It also learns from the plans the coach publishes. There is nothing to set up: one button to upload, one to use it.

Upload once

Practice › Plan format, also linked from the line "In your format · Fall practice sheet" at the bottom of the ask first card. Takes a PDF, a photo of a printed sheet, or a Word or Excel file. While it reads, a scan line passes down the page and the same steps as Ask tick off. Then it shows the five parts it found, outlined on the page and listed in plain words, with Use this format. That's the whole setup.

It copies the layout, not the old content

It keeps the sections, their order and the columns of the schedule. The drills, times and groups in a new plan still come from the coach's data and answers, never from the old sheet.

It learns from every plan, with no settings

Each plan the coach publishes, past and future, teaches it: drills kept and swapped out, how long blocks run, how the groups are split. Those become its best guesses in the ask first card ("You split into 3 groups most weeks"). No training step, no toggles.

Kept simple

No template editor and no field mapping. If the coach doesn't like it, Try another or go back to the GolfHelm layout. Undo is there for 8 seconds after saving.

Scene 4 · Practice › New plan › Builder

A helper inside the wizard, never in charge of it

The builder keeps its four steps (Blocks, Groups, Drills, Review). CoachHelm offers what fits the step you're on. Its suggestions arrive as outlined drafts with Keep, Swap and Remove, and nothing becomes part of the plan until the coach keeps it.

What it offers, step by step

  • New plan sheet: "Start from" gains a fourth choice, "Ask CoachHelm", with suggested focuses taken from the data ("Putts inside 6 feet", "Wedges inside 100").
  • Blocks, empty: the green helper card with three facts and "Fill the draft".
  • Blocks, one block selected: "Suggest a drill for this station" and "Fit this to 20 minutes".
  • Groups: "Group by need" (who loses most from 3 to 6 feet) or "Keep late arrivals together", each grouping with its reason.
  • Drills: per station, the three best fits from the bank, each with its because.
  • Review: "Check balance": minutes per area against where the team loses strokes, in one line ("45 of 135 minutes on putting, the team's biggest loss").

The feature card is the helper

On an empty plan the helper is the screen's one feature card. Once the coach keeps or dismisses the suggestions it folds into a quiet row, "CoachHelm · 6 suggestions kept", so the plan becomes the hero.

Ghost blocks

Suggested blocks sit on the well colour with a green outline and the word "Suggested". Keep turns one solid with the card shadow. Keep all does every one at once. Nothing applies in the background, and a suggestion never overwrites a block the coach made.

Scene 5 · What players get

One notification, their own start

Publish sends each player one push and one bell row, worded for them. Tapping it opens their path through the practice. The plan also lives in Team Hub › Documents › Practice plans.

Exactly what is sent

Title "Practice plan for Thursday". Body "2:00 PM · You start on the putting green with Group B". Push in the Events category, so each player's own switch and Quiet mode apply, plus a bell row. Only Publish, a republish with changes to their day, or Take down sends anything (the practice spec's three presses).

Players see the plan, not the reasoning

The why lines are in the coach's copy only (settled with Nick). Players see the focus, their group and their stops.

Seen by

Back in Ask, the receipt shows "Seen by 4 of 9" as players open the plan, with names on tap.

The evidence rule, applied to plans

Every block says why, or says it's routine

The CoachHelm evidence rule stays strict. A block's reason is built from reads made in this turn, and the numbers in it must match those reads. The research pattern we took is Garmin Coach's three parts: what it's for, what you do, and because.

Aim

One line, what the block is for. "More makes from 3 to 6 feet."

Because

One sentence with the figure, the comparison and the window. "The team makes 71% from 3 to 6 feet; the Tour line is 84%. Last 10 team rounds, 412 putts."

Source chips

Up to two, each naming where it came from: "Stats · Putting by distance", "Calendar · Thu", "Classes". A chip opens the evidence panel CoachHelm already has, with the rounds behind it.

Routine

Warm-up, moving between places and the wrap carry no claim. They read "Routine", unless real data shaped them ("Starts 2:30 so Ava and Jade make every turn · Classes").

Thin data

Under 5 rounds in the window, the block says so ("Only 3 rounds since Oct 1, so this is a lean read") instead of a confident number. No data at all, no because.

Drills come from the bank

CoachHelm picks only from the team's drill bank and the GolfHelm library, by id. If nothing fits, it says so and offers "New drill" for the coach to write. It never invents a drill.

Motion lab

Every motion, with its Play button

All curves are the shared ones in lib/motion.ts and tokens.css. Only transform and opacity move. No count-ups and no staggers on page load: blocks arrive one at a time because they are made one at a time. Turn on Reduce Motion above to see each one's quiet version.

Contrast, light and dark

Text holds 4.9:1 or better on the surface it sits on

Worked out live in this page from the tokens it uses (WCAG 2.x relative luminance). Chart and strip marks need 3:1 against their track.

Research

What we took, and what we left

Claude artifacts, ChatGPT canvas

Compact card in the chat, the full thing in a panel; edit by asking or by touching it; versions one tap back. Claude help, OpenAI

Garmin Coach

Every suggestion explains itself as goal, action, reason. That became Aim and Because. the5krunner

TrainingPeaks

Blocks on a strip where width is time, and live totals as you edit. That became the day strip and the ribbon. TrainingPeaks help

Notion AI

What the assistant offers depends on where you are. That became the builder helper's per-step offers. Notion

Runna, WHOOP

Named presets beat free text for adjusting ("Not feeling 100%"), and plans change because of data, saying so. That became the refine chips. Runna, Wareable

Apple Fitness+ Custom Plans

Few inputs, an editable review, one verb on the commit button. That became "Publish and notify 11". Apple

Left out

Fitbod's silent background refresh (a draft never changes without the coach seeing it), counting-up totals (banned here), streaks, and long onboarding before the first draft.

Settled with Nick, 31 Oct

The four calls, decided

1. When it ships

Settled: together with the Practice tab, right after Practice slices 1 to 5.

2. Do players see the why lines?

Settled: no. The coach copy has them; players get the same sheet without them, plus their group and their stops.

3. Where a saved draft lives

Settled: in Documents, marked "Only coaches", and "Shared with players" once published.

4. Drills it may use

Settled: only drills from the team's bank and the GolfHelm library. When nothing fits it says so and opens New drill.

And from his notes

Plans show groups throughout. Coaches can upload a sample plan as the format, and CoachHelm learns from past and future plans. Keep it simple: no extra settings for coaches.
